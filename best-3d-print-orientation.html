Best 3D Print Orientation: How to Choose the Right Way Up

The same model can be strong or fragile, smooth or rough, quick or slow to print, depending only on how it lies on the bed. Orientation is the cheapest improvement you can make, because it costs nothing but a few minutes of thought. This guide gives a method for choosing it.

What orientation changes

  • Strength. FDM parts are strongest along the layers and weakest between them. A part loaded so that layers are pulled apart will snap along a layer line.
  • Surface quality. Vertical walls come out smooth. Curved and sloped surfaces show stair-stepping, which is worst on shallow slopes. The bottom face, which touches the bed, is flat and often shiny.
  • Supports. Any surface that faces downward at a steep overhang needs support, and the face touched by support is rougher.
  • Time and material. Tall prints need more layers; flat prints use more first-layer area. Supports add both.
  • Accuracy. Holes printed on their side tend to sag into an oval. Standing them upright keeps them round.

A five-step method

  1. Find the largest flat face. It is the natural bottom. A wide flat base sticks well and needs no support.
  2. Locate the load. Imagine where force acts on the part. Orient it so layers are not being pulled apart by that force; tall thin parts such as hooks and clips are usually strongest when printed lying down.
  3. Locate the showcase surface. Put the face people will see on a vertical wall or on the top, not on a support.
  4. Count overhangs. As a rule of thumb, overhangs steeper than about 45 degrees from vertical often need support. Rotate the model and compare the amount of support in the slicer preview.
  5. Check the first layer. A tiny contact area warps or detaches. Add a brim or choose another face.

Orientation for the preview, too

The orientation you print in is not always the best for a preview. In the converter, rotate the model to its natural display pose, the way a buyer would put it on a shelf. Check the up axis first; a mismatch makes the model lie down. See Why Your STL Looks Too Small, Too Big or Upside Down.

Frequently asked questions

Should I always avoid supports?

No. Supports are fine when the visible surface is protected. Avoiding them completely can force a worse orientation.

Does orientation change the file?

The slicer only rotates the model for printing. Your STL file is unchanged unless you export it again.
