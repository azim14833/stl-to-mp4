Choosing a Scene and Background for 3D Model Previews

The background changes how a model is read. A plain colour says "product", a photographed desk says "real object", and a 3D room says "showroom". None is always best. This guide helps you pick one and avoid the most common reason previews look fake: an object that does not sit on its surface.

Plain colours and gradients

Safest choice. They keep attention on the shape, never clash with the model's colour and compress well in video. Use a mid-tone grey or a soft gradient. Avoid pure white for white or light models and pure black for dark ones, because edges vanish.

Photographed scenes

A photo of a desk, shelf or windowsill gives instant context and scale. It works best when three things agree:

  • Contact. The base of the model must touch the visible surface, with a soft shadow beneath it.
  • Perspective. The camera elevation should match the photo. A desk shot from slightly above needs a camera of roughly 20 to 28 degrees. A top-down camera over a side-on photo looks wrong.
  • Light direction. Shadows should fall the same way as in the picture. If the window is on the right, the shadow goes left.

3D rooms

A simple 3D environment such as a table in front of a wall responds correctly when the camera moves, so it suits camera tours. Keep the blur on so the room supports the object rather than competing with it.

Transparent background

Useful for stills you will place on your own artwork. MP4 does not normally store transparency, so video needs a solid background.

Sizing for phones

On a phone the preview area is tall and narrow. A 16:9 frame uses only a thin strip of the screen, so the model looks small. Choose a 4:5 or 9:16 output size and the preview fills the screen; the model is also framed to fill most of the width. Remember that the final video is exactly the size you choose, so pick the format of the platform where you will post it.

Checklist before exporting

  1. Does the object touch the surface?
  2. Is the shadow direction consistent with the background?
  3. Is there enough contrast between model and scene?
  4. Is the model large enough to read at thumbnail size?
  5. Is the background calm enough to be forgotten?

Frequently asked questions

Why does my model look like it is floating?

Usually the shadow is off, the camera elevation does not match the photo, or the model is placed too high. Turn shadows on, use an elevation of about 18 to 24 degrees and lower the model with the vertical position slider.

Can I use my own photo?

Yes. Pick a photo you own or have the right to use, ideally one with a visible flat surface in the lower half.
