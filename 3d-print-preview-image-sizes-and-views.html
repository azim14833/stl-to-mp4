Best Image Sizes and Views for 3D Print Listings and Social Media

Good presentation sells 3D models. Two designs of equal quality will not attract equal attention if one is shown with a blurry, badly cropped picture. This guide explains which views buyers expect to see, which image sizes suit the places you will post them, and how to keep your whole catalogue consistent.

The views buyers expect

A complete image set answers the questions a buyer would otherwise ask you in a message. Aim for this set, in a fixed order:

  1. Hero shot: a shaded perspective view from the front and slightly above. This is the image that appears in search results, so it must work as a thumbnail.
  2. Orthographic view: shows true proportions without perspective distortion, useful for judging size relationships.
  3. Front, right, left and back views: so no side stays hidden.
  4. Top view: reveals the footprint and symmetrical details.
  5. Bottom view: proves the model has a flat base and no hidden problems. Many sellers skip it and many buyers look for it.
  6. Wireframe: an extra that shows mesh density and helps technical buyers judge printability.

Platforms change their limits and cropping rules from time to time, so read the upload guidelines of each site you use before you prepare a whole catalogue. When unsure, export at 1920 × 1080 or higher and crop down; it is easy to shrink an image and impossible to add real detail to a small one.

Choosing a file format

  • PNG keeps edges crisp and supports transparency, which is ideal when you place the model on your own banner or collage. Files are larger.
  • JPG produces much smaller files and suits photographic backgrounds. Save once from the original; repeated saves add compression artefacts.
  • WebP offers a good balance of size and quality for websites and supports transparency.

Framing and composition

Leave a margin of about 10 percent around the model so it does not touch the edge. Keep the model centred and the camera distance the same across angles, so thumbnails look uniform when they sit side by side. Use a neutral background and a soft shadow. If the model is tall and thin, a portrait ratio such as 4:5 uses the frame better than a wide landscape one.

Consistency builds trust

Use the same background, lighting and camera distance across all of your listings. A shop with uniform previews looks organised and reliable, and customers can compare items quickly. Add the model's name and real size as a caption when the platform allows it; buyers want to know whether a figure is 5 cm or 25 cm tall before they commit filament and time.

Be honest about renders

A render shows the digital model, not your printer's result. Label renders clearly and add real photos of physical prints whenever you have them. Honest previews reduce complaints and refund requests, and they protect your reputation over the long term.

Checklist before you publish

  • Hero image looks good at thumbnail size.
  • All sides and the bottom are shown.
  • Sizes match the platform's current requirements.
  • Real photos and renders are labelled as such.
  • Background and lighting match the rest of your shop.

Resolution and sharpness

A larger image is not automatically a sharper one. What matters is whether the model's detail survives the size at which people see it. A thumbnail in search results is often smaller than 400 pixels wide, so the silhouette and a single strong colour or shadow decide whether anyone clicks. Check each hero image at thumbnail size before you publish; if you cannot recognise the object, change the angle, zoom in a little, or simplify the background.

For detail shots, export at the highest resolution your device handles and crop in afterwards. A 3840 × 2160 image gives you room to crop a close-up of engraved text without losing quality.

Naming and organising your files

Name images in a way that keeps the set in order, for example dragon-01-shaded.png, dragon-02-front.png and so on. Numbered files make uploads quicker, keep marketplaces from shuffling the order, and help you find the right file months later. Keep an uncompressed master set and create smaller copies for upload.

Frequently asked questions

How many images should a listing have?

Between five and ten covers most models: hero, orthographic, two or three angles, a bottom view, a wireframe, a size reference and one or two real photos.

Should the background be white?

White works for stylised catalogues, but light models vanish on it. A soft grey or a muted colour gives more contrast for most filament colours.

Can I reuse the same images on every platform?

Yes, if the ratios fit. Create a square, a landscape and a portrait version of your hero and reuse them. Always check each platform's current rules first.
