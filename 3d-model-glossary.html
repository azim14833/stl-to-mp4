3D Model, Rendering and Video Glossary

Short definitions of terms that appear when you prepare 3D models for printing, rendering and video. Use it as a quick reference alongside the other guides.

A

Ambient occlusion

Darkening of crevices where light would not reach easily.

Aspect ratio

Width divided by height, such as 16:9, 4:5 or 9:16.

B

Bitrate

Data used per second of video. Higher means better quality and bigger files.

Bounding box

The smallest box that contains the model.

C

Camera tour

A video in which the camera moves to several angles around the object.

Contact shadow

A soft dark area right under an object that makes it look grounded.

D

Decimation

Reducing the number of triangles while keeping the shape.

E

Elevation

The angle of the camera above the horizontal plane.

F

Field of view (FOV)

How wide the camera sees. Wide values exaggerate perspective.

Flipped normals

Faces whose normals point inward, causing dark or inside-out shading.

Frame rate (fps)

Frames shown per second. 24 to 30 is usual for previews.

G

GIF

An old image format with animation support, limited to 256 colours.

H

H.264

A widely supported video codec used inside most MP4 files.

L

Lens shift

Moving the image sideways or up and down without tilting the camera.

M

MP4

A container format that stores video (and audio) streams.

Manifold (watertight)

A mesh with no holes, so every edge is shared by exactly two faces. Slicers prefer it.

Material

Settings that define colour, shininess and metalness.

Mesh

A collection of vertices, edges and faces that forms a 3D surface.

Metalness

Whether a surface behaves like metal in reflections.

Muxer

Software that packs encoded video into a container such as MP4.

N

Normal

A direction perpendicular to a surface, used to calculate shading.

O

OBJ

A text-based 3D format that can store vertices, faces, normals and texture coordinates.

Orthographic view

A projection without perspective, used for technical views.

Overhang

A part of the model that extends outward without material below.

P

Perspective

The effect that makes distant objects look smaller.

R

Roughness

How matte or glossy a surface looks.

S

STL

A file format that describes a surface as a list of triangles. It stores no colour and no units.

Shadow map

A texture the renderer uses to decide which areas are in shadow.

Slicer

Software that turns a model into layers and instructions for a 3D printer.

Support structure

Temporary material that holds up overhangs during printing.

T

Tone mapping

Conversion of bright lighting values to what a screen can display.

Triangle count

The number of triangles in a mesh. Higher counts mean more detail and more memory.

Turntable

A video in which the object rotates while the camera is fixed.

U

Up axis

The axis that points up in the file, often Y or Z. A mismatch makes models lie down.

V

Vertex

A point in 3D space where edges meet.

W

WebCodecs

A browser interface that gives pages access to the device video encoder.

WebGL

A browser interface for drawing 3D graphics on the graphics card.

WebM

An open container often used with VP8 or VP9 video.
