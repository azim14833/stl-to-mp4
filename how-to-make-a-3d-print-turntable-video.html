How to Make a Turntable Video of a 3D Model

A short rotating video shows the shape of a 3D model far better than a single still image. Buyers can see the back, the overhangs and the proportions without guessing. This guide covers the choices that decide whether a turntable clip looks professional or amateur.

1. Prepare the model first

Rotate the model so its base is flat and its most interesting side faces the camera at the start. If the model was exported with the wrong up axis, correct it before recording, otherwise it will tumble end over end instead of spinning on its base. Centre the model so it rotates around its own middle rather than wobbling off to one side.

2. Choose a calm background

The model should be the hero. A soft grey gradient, a plain studio floor or a gentle shadow works better than a busy scene. Make sure there is enough contrast: a white model on a white background disappears, and a black model on a dark scene loses its detail. Light from one side at a slight angle so the surface texture and layer details create depth.

3. Pick a camera angle

An elevation of about 20 to 30 degrees looks natural, as if you were looking down at a model on a desk. Straight-on side views flatten the shape, while steep top-down views hide the silhouette. Leave a little empty space around the model so it never touches the edge of the frame while rotating.

4. Set duration and loops

One full rotation in 6 to 10 seconds feels smooth. Faster spins look nervous and make detail hard to read; slower spins feel boring and inflate the file size. Rotating exactly 360 degrees makes the clip loop seamlessly, which matters on social platforms that replay videos automatically.

5. Resolution, frame rate and bitrate

  • 1920 x 1080 at 30 fps is a safe default for marketplaces and YouTube.
  • 1080 x 1080 suits square feeds; 1080 x 1920 suits Reels, TikTok and Shorts.
  • A bitrate around 10 to 15 Mbps keeps fine surface detail sharp without producing a huge file.

6. Check the result

Watch the finished video on a phone, not only on your monitor. If thin parts flicker, if the shadow jumps, or if the file is larger than the upload limit of your platform, lower the bitrate or shorten the clip and export again. A few minutes of review is cheaper than replacing a weak preview later.
