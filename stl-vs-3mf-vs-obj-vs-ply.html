STL vs 3MF vs OBJ vs PLY: Which 3D File Format Should You Use?

If you sell, share or print 3D models you will meet four file formats again and again: STL, 3MF, OBJ and PLY. They look interchangeable because most slicers open all of them, but they store very different information. Choosing the right one saves you from lost colours, wrong sizes and bloated downloads.

STL: the universal baseline

STL describes a surface as a list of triangles and nothing else. There is no colour, no material and, importantly, no unit. A file that is 40 units wide may be 40 mm or 40 inches; the slicer simply assumes millimetres. STL exists in binary and ASCII flavours. Binary is far smaller and is what nearly every tool exports. Use STL when you want maximum compatibility and your model is a single-colour solid.

3MF: the modern replacement

3MF is a zipped package that stores the mesh together with units, colours, multiple objects, build plate positions and often slicer settings. Because the unit is stored, a 3MF file does not suffer from the scale confusion that plagues STL. It is also usually smaller than the equivalent STL. If your slicer and your audience support it, 3MF is the better format for multi-part or multi-colour projects.

OBJ: meshes with materials

OBJ is an older text format that can carry texture coordinates and normals, and it references a companion .mtl file for materials. It is common in modelling and game tools. For printing it works, but the separate material file is easy to lose, and the text encoding makes large models heavy. Pick OBJ when you need textures or are moving a model between sculpting and rendering software.

PLY: scans and vertex colour

PLY was designed for 3D scanners. It can store colour directly on vertices, which makes it popular for photogrammetry and full-colour scans. It handles dense meshes well, but support in print-focused tools is less consistent than STL or 3MF.

Quick decision guide

  • Single-colour print, maximum compatibility: binary STL.
  • Multi-part or multi-colour print, correct size guaranteed: 3MF.
  • Textured model or hand-off between art tools: OBJ with its .mtl file.
  • Scanned or vertex-coloured model: PLY.

Checking a file before you publish it

Before you upload a model to a marketplace, open it in a viewer and confirm three things: the model sits flat on the build plate, the dimensions match what you advertise, and the colours or parts you expect are present. A quick turntable preview reveals flipped faces, missing parts and strange orientation immediately, which is far cheaper than discovering them from a buyer's complaint.
