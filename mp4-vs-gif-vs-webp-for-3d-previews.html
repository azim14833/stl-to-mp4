MP4 vs GIF vs WebP: Which Format for 3D Model Previews?

Once you have a good turntable animation, you have to choose how to save it. MP4, GIF and animated WebP all show a rotating model, but they differ a great deal in size, colour quality and where they are accepted. Choosing well can cut a file to a tenth of its size or save a listing from being rejected. This guide compares the three and tells you when to use each.

MP4: the default for quality

MP4 with H.264 video is the best general choice. It handles smooth gradients, soft shadows and fine surface texture, which are exactly what a 3D render is made of. It compresses motion efficiently, so a 1080p, 8-second turntable at 6 Mbps is only about 6 MB. Almost every video platform and social network accepts it, and many marketplaces allow video in the main gallery. Its limits: it normally has no transparency, and a few image-only upload forms refuse it.

GIF: universal, but heavy

GIF has one great strength, which is that it plays almost anywhere an image can be uploaded. It also has big weaknesses. A palette of 256 colours per frame turns soft gradients into visible bands and makes glossy materials look blotchy. Frames are compressed poorly, so a short clip can reach tens of megabytes. Transparency is on or off with no smooth edges, so a model on a transparent GIF often shows a jagged halo. Use GIF only where nothing else is accepted, and keep it small: 480 to 720 pixels wide, 12 to 15 frames per second, a simple background.

Animated WebP: a good middle path

Animated WebP supports millions of colours and full transparency at much smaller sizes than GIF. It is a good fit for your own website, where you want a looping preview without a video player. It is less widely accepted by marketplaces and social platforms, and older software may not display it. Test in the browsers your audience actually uses.

Choosing by destination

  • Marketplace listing with video support: MP4, 1920 × 1080, 6 Mbps.
  • Marketplace image gallery only: GIF, small and short, plus still images.
  • Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts: MP4, 1080 × 1920.
  • Your own website: MP4 in a video tag with muted autoplay and loop, or animated WebP.
  • Forum posts and chat: GIF or short MP4, depending on what the platform previews.

Reducing the size of any format

  1. Shorten the clip to a single revolution of about 6 seconds.
  2. Lower the resolution before you lower quality: 1280 × 720 looks fine on a phone.
  3. Use a simple background; fine patterns and noise cost a lot of bits.
  4. Lower the frame rate to 24 fps for slow rotations.
  5. For GIF, reduce the palette and use fewer frames.

Always keep stills as well

Even where animation is allowed, some viewers will not play it, and search engines and shop previews often show only the first frame. Provide a strong still hero image and the standard view set alongside the animation, so every visitor sees the model clearly regardless of format.
