How to Photograph 3D Prints: Simple Setup, Light and Backgrounds

Rendered previews show a design; photographs prove that it exists and prints well. Buyers trust listings that show both. You do not need special equipment: a phone, a window and a few sheets of paper are enough.

Choose the light

Soft daylight beside a window gives the most pleasant result. Place the print so that the light comes from the side, not from behind the camera, to show layer detail and shape. Direct sun creates harsh shadows and strong highlights on glossy filament; if the sun is out, diffuse it with a white curtain or a sheet of baking paper.

Pick a clean background

A sheet of white or light grey paper curved up behind the print makes a seamless backdrop. Dark prints look good on a light surface, and light prints on a mid-tone one. Avoid patterned cloths, which fight with the surface texture.

Show the surface honestly

  • Photograph the real colour. Auto white balance sometimes shifts it, so compare with the print on screen.
  • Include one close-up that shows the layer lines. Honest detail builds trust.
  • Clean off dust, strings and support marks before shooting.

Give a sense of scale

Place a coin, a hand or a ruler next to the object in one image. Dimensions in the listing text are helpful, but a visual reference is faster to understand.

Angles

  1. Three-quarter view from slightly above, the main image.
  2. Straight front view.
  3. A close-up of the best detail.
  4. A top view if the design has a pattern or text.
  5. A scale shot.

Phone camera tips

  • Clean the lens.
  • Tap the print to focus and lower the exposure slightly if the surface is shiny.
  • Step back and zoom in a little rather than shooting very close, to reduce distortion.
  • Use a tripod or a stack of books to keep the phone steady.

Combining photos and renders

Use the rendered turntable or camera tour to show every side, and photographs to show the real material. In the converter, a photographed desk or shelf scene gives renders a similar look; see Choosing a Scene and Background.

Frequently asked questions

Should the photos replace the renders?

No. Renders show clean geometry from all angles; photos show real colour and finish. The combination works best.
